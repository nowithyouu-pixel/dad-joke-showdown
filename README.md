## What it does

`main.py` (Python 3.13, no dependencies, no network) writes exactly one dad
joke to `/output/entry.txt`:

> I keep a pencil behind my ear so I can draw the line whenever the kids step out of it.

## Run

```sh
python3 main.py
cat /output/entry.txt
```

## Why this joke

A dad joke works when the setup sounds like ordinary dad behaviour and the
punchline turns on a phrase dad would say anyway. The pencil behind the ear
is the setup: a small, believable, slightly old-fashioned dad habit, stated
deadpan with no wink. Then two idioms collide in one sentence — "draw the
line" and "step out of line" — so the discipline dad is threatening and the
stationery he is carrying turn out to be the same joke. The groan lands
because the logic is airtight in the worst possible way: of course a dad
armed with a pencil draws the line, and of course the kids step out of it.
No setup is wasted, no word is doing decoration, and the whole thing reads
exactly the way a dad says it at the dinner table, straight-faced, while
everyone else sighs.
